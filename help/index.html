How to play

Four rules. ገበጣ in two minutes.

Gebeta looks intimidating the first time someone's grandmother wins in four moves. It isn't — it's four rules stacked on top of each other. Here's each one, in order, with the board as it actually looks in the app.

01

The board

Two rows of six pits. The row closest to you is yours; the row across from you is your opponent's. Every pit starts with 4 seeds — 48 seeds total, and they never leave the game, they just move between pits or into a captured pile.

There's no "home store" like some mancala games — captured seeds go straight to your score. Whoever has captured more than 24 of the 48 when the game ends wins.

02

Making a move ("sowing")

On your turn, pick any pit on your side that has at least one seed. Scoop up everything in it, then drop one seed into each pit going clockwise around the whole board — across your row, up into your opponent's row, across theirs, and back to yours if you have enough seeds to make it all the way around. (If you do lap all the way back, you skip the pit you started from — you never drop a seed back into the hole you just emptied.)

Think of the two rows as one continuous loop, not two separate lines. The app mirrors the opponent's row on screen specifically so this loop reads left-to-right-then-back, matching the physical board.
03

Capturing seeds

This is the only genuinely tricky part, and it's one rule with a backup clause:

Main rule — if the very last seed you drop lands in one of your opponent's pits and that pit now holds exactly 2 or 3 seeds, you capture the whole pit. Then look at the pit immediately before it (the one you filled just before this one) — if that one is also sitting at 2 or 3, capture that too, and keep walking backward as long as the streak holds.

Backup rule — if the main rule didn't trigger (your last seed landed on your opponent's side but that pit wasn't at 2 or 3, or on an empty pit of theirs), but it did land in one of your own pits and left it at 2 or 3 — you capture just that one pit.

04

You can't starve your opponent

A move that would leave your opponent with zero seeds anywhere in their row (after captures resolve) is illegal — unless it's genuinely the only move you have, in which case it's forced. And if your opponent's row is already empty, you must play a move that gives them seeds; you can't just stall.

If you ever have no legal move at all, you pass and the turn goes back to your opponent. Two passes in a row (the board's frozen for both sides) ends the game. As a backstop, 50 moves without a single capture also ends it — the score stands as-is, so a cautious game can't stall forever.

If you remember nothing else

  • Pick a pit on your side, sow one seed per pit clockwise around the whole board.
  • Land your last seed on an opponent's pit that's now at 2 or 3 → capture it, and keep capturing backward while the streak holds.
  • No capture there, but your last seed left one of your pits at 2 or 3 → capture just that one.
  • Never leave your opponent with nothing, unless you truly have no other option.
  • More than 24 of the 48 seeds captured wins. 24–24 is a draw.

The AI's easy difficulty is genuinely easy. Go lose to it a few times first.
